Supervisors of security operations centres (SOCs) must periodically judge whether
monitored organisations detect, investigate, escalate and close security work
properly, yet common instruments are self-assessed maturity models and manual
record review. We describe SAT-SA, a system that analyses the operational records
an organisation submits each period (alerts, cases, investigation steps,
escalations, dispositions, assets), produces evidence-cited supervisory findings
from \V{CODE-workers} deterministic analytical workers, fuses them into an entity
risk and priority, requires a human supervisory decision, and binds the reviewed
state with an \V{CODE-signature} signed, hash-chained receipt. We evaluate it with
frozen, hash-verified experiment bundles. In \V{C01-action-total} controlled scenarios SAT-SA
emitted every declared finding family (recall \V{C01-recall}, precision
\V{C01-precision}); simple fixed-threshold and median-absolute-deviation rules tied
its fast-closure detector. Across \V{PR01-replicates} generated populations its ranking exceeded
random and alert-volume ordering, but a closure-speed heuristic was comparable.
Hosted validation matched its declared contract in all \V{R01b-expect} perturbed
conditions, while stale and contradictory records were accepted silently. The
integrity layer detected all \V{T01-detected} tested mutations; durable orchestration
added a median \V{O01-diff-proc}\,s per workflow on one machine (95\% interval
\V{O01-diff-proc-lo} to \V{O01-diff-proc-hi}\,s) and all \V{O02-runs} injected
interruptions recovered. On an external, real IT incident log (not SOC data) the
pipeline ran without rejecting any record, but SAT-SA risk was not associated with
service-level misses (Spearman $\rho=\V{X02b-rho-risk}$); a failure analysis
attributes this mainly to construct mismatch. The evidence supports mechanism-level
claims under controlled conditions and feasibility on external data, not
real-world SOC effectiveness; no human study or expert labels exist, and production
deployment is unverified.
